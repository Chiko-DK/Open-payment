Problem Statement
In South Africa, the average low-income earner, defined as someone earning approximately R5,000 per month or relying on a SASSA grant of about R2,330, typically faces a financial shortfall where monthly expenses exceed income. Due to financial exclusion, these individuals often do not have access to formal bank loans. This exclusion may be voluntary, stemming from mistrust of the banking sector or cultural beliefs, or involuntary, due to incomes too low to qualify for or sustain formal financial products (Chitimira & Ncube, 2020).
As a result, many low-income earners resort to borrowing from informal and often illegal lenders such as loan sharks (mashonisas) or pseudo-banks. These lenders operate outside the bounds of legal and financial regulation. Transactions with such lenders are unrecorded and do not contribute to building a credit history. (James, 2021)
Moreover, these lending practices are frequently exploitative and unsafe: borrowers are charged exorbitant interest rates and may be required to hand over their bank cards or identification cards as collateral (James, 2021). In cases of non-payment, intimidation, scare tactics, and even violence are common.
Because these transactions are not formally tracked, borrowers are left without any legal recourse or traceable financial footprint. This further entrenches financial invisibility and vulnerability, limiting opportunities for upward mobility and reinforcing cycles of poverty.
Why is the team tackling this problem, what are the benefits for lenders, borrowers?
Team VulaPay wants to enable low-income earners to have access to safe and affordable loans, without the risk of exploitation.
This would benefit both lenders and borrowers. These previously excluded borrowers now have access to safe, small loans with less stringent requirements than formal banks.These loans are traceable, thus building a credit profile. And these loans will have flexible terms and lower interest rates compared to illegal lenders.
Some benefits for lenders include the adjustable terms such as interest rates and timeframes. Lenders have lower operational fees than traditional lending platforms, and the ability to diversify risk by spreading small loans across many borrowers.
Our Solution: LowN – A Peer-to-Peer Lending Platform
LowN is a digital peer-to-peer loan system designed to give low-income earners access to small, affordable loans. Users connect their bank accounts and request loans directly from a list of available lenders. Lenders set their preferred loan amounts, interest rates, and repayment periods, when both parties approve the agreement is finalised. Loans start from as little as R100, with flexible weekly repayment options, making borrowing accessible and manageable. Borrowers can compare offers, while lenders have visibility into borrower requests and profiles.
What Makes LowN Unique
LowN is built specifically for low-income users and focuses on eliminating intermediaries to reduce costs. It offers transparency, fair terms, and a system that discourages exploitative lending. Lenders have the freedom to select borrowers and set terms within defined limits, with safeguards (currently in development) to ensure fairness. The platform prioritizes trust, affordability, and financial inclusion—empowering users to safely participate in the credit system.
The future
